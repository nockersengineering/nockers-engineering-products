# Hot and Cold Cutting Wires

Hot and cold cutting wires are used for a wide range of industrial cutting processes. Their applications extend from shaping rigid foam and sealing polythene bags to abrasive cutting and food-grade slicing.

## Hot wire cutting

Hot wire cutting is commonly used on rigid foam materials such as expanded polystyrene (EPS) and expanded polyurethane (EPU).

A rigid cutting wire is connected across a variable low-voltage supply, heating it to a temperature suitable for the material being cut. The operating temperature and feed speed must be balanced carefully.

If the temperature is too low, or the feed speed is too slow, melted material can re-harden in the kerf and leave a ragged cut. Excessive temperature can stretch the wire and shorten its service life.

## Materials used for hot cutting

The wire material depends on the machine parameters, wire configuration, and length of cut. Options range from stainless steel to nickel-chrome alloys. Some cutting wires use a copper coating to help reduce sticking.

Similar heating wires are also used to cut and seal polythene bags. Composite-profile wires can perform the cutting and sealing operations at the same time.

## Cold cutting wires

Cold cutting wires perform abrasive cutting as either a rigid wire knife or a rotating wire band saw.

Typical materials include high-tensile carbon steel and high-tensile alloy steel containing chromium or manganese. Food applications such as cheese cutting generally require high-tensile stainless steel wire.

High-density materials, long cuts, and profile cuts may require a strand made from several wires. Specialised constructions can combine a harder cutting wrap wire with a more resilient multi-wire core.
