# Spring Wire Materials: How to Match Grade, Finish and Application

![Spring wire](https://nockers.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Rectangle-9-copy-2-26.webp)

Spring wire selection starts with the operating conditions of the finished component. Load cycles, temperature, corrosion exposure, conductivity, forming method and cost can all change which material is appropriate.

A useful specification therefore includes more than diameter and tensile strength. Engineers and buyers should also confirm the material grade, finish, heat treatment, dimensional tolerance, required documentation and supply format.

## Five main spring-wire material groups

The [Nockers spring wire range](https://nockers.co.za/products/spring-wire/) covers five main material categories, each intended for different mechanical loads, environments and service-life requirements.

### 1. Carbon steel spring wire

Carbon steel is widely used because it combines strength with cost efficiency. Hard-drawn material is suitable for many standard applications, while patented-drawn and phosphate-coated options are used where enhanced fatigue performance or surface quality is required.

Available finishes and variants listed by Nockers include black, grey phosphate, zinc, copper, tin and nickel, together with hard-drawn, induction-heated-and-tempered, oil-tempered, polished and galvanised options.

Typical uses include springs, clips and shaped wire parts. The correct grade and finish depend on the forming process, load cycle and working environment.

### 2. Alloy steel spring wire

Alloy steel wire is used for demanding dynamic applications. Silicon, chromium or vanadium additions are used to support fatigue life and strength retention. Supply options include oil-hardened-and-tempered and induction-hardened-and-tempered wire.

Typical applications include automotive or industrial engine valve springs and heavy-duty axle springs for transport and machinery.

### 3. Stainless steel spring wire

Stainless steel is selected where moisture, humidity or mineral salts make corrosion resistance important. Nockers identifies AISI 302 (1.4310) as the most widely used grade, with AISI 316 (1.4401) and 17/7PH (1.4568) available for more demanding environments and elevated-temperature applications up to 300 °C.

Listed finishes include bright, dull or soap-coated, nickel-coated bright, nickel-coated dull and straightened wire. Applications include marine equipment, food and beverage processing, and chemical handling.

### 4. Copper-alloy spring wire

Copper-alloy materials are useful where electrical conductivity, solderability or non-sparking performance matters. Options listed by Nockers include phosphor bronze and beryllium copper.

High-copper alloys can also be selected for springs used in gaseous or flammable environments where non-sparking properties are required. The application, regulatory requirements and alloy suitability should be confirmed before selection.

### 5. Nickel-alloy spring wire

Nickel-alloy wire is intended for severe temperature or corrosion conditions. Nockers lists specialised grades including Inconel 718, Inconel X750, Nimonic 80A and Nimonic 90.

Potential applications include gas turbines, aerospace components and nuclear reactors. These are specialised, safety-critical uses, so the exact grade, standard, certification and operating limits must be confirmed by the responsible engineer.

## What to include in a spring-wire enquiry

A clear enquiry helps the supplier identify suitable options. Useful information includes:

- required material or applicable standard;
- wire diameter and dimensional tolerance;
- tensile-strength or mechanical-property requirements;
- operating temperature and corrosion exposure;
- expected load cycles and fatigue requirements;
- surface finish, coating or heat treatment;
- coil, spool or other packaging requirements; and
- material certificates or traceability documents required.

Nockers publishes technical information for stainless, unalloyed carbon-steel, and oil-hardened-and-tempered spring wire, including EN 10270 material references. The exact revision and applicability of a standard should be confirmed for each order.
