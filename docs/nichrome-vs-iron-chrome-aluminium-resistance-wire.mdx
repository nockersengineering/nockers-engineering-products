# Nichrome vs Iron-Chrome-Aluminium Resistance Wire

NOCKERS ENGINEERING PRODUCTS supplies resistance wire (or heating wire) in two different grades: Nickel-Chrome (Nichrome) Ni80Cr20 and Iron-Chrome-Aluminium 0Cr23Al5.

The suitability of each grade depends on the application. The comparison below highlights the main characteristics of each.

## Comparison of Resistance Wire Grades

### Working temperature

- Ni80Cr20: Up to 1200°C
- 0Cr23Al5: Up to 1400°C

### Hot and creep strength

Ni80Cr20: Higher

### Ductility after long use

- Ni80Cr20: Better ductility after long use
- 0Cr23Al5: Becomes brittle after long use

### Element temperature

Ni80Cr20: Lower for the same surface loading

### Design life and surface loading

0Cr23Al5: Longer design life and higher working temperature allow higher surface loading

### Corrosion resistance at room temperature

Ni80Cr20: Better

### Resistance to sulphur

0Cr23Al5: Better

### Surface oxidation properties

0Cr23Al5: Better

### Density and resistivity

0Cr23Al5: Lower density and higher resistivity provide weight savings for a given load

### Yield strength

0Cr23Al5: Higher yield strength reduces cross-sectional change during severe coiling

### Magnetic properties

- Ni80Cr20: Non-magnetic
- 0Cr23Al5: Magnetic below 600°C

### Surface characteristics for heat sealing

Ni80Cr20: Better

### Cost

- Ni80Cr20: Higher, due to nickel content
- 0Cr23Al5: Lower

### Typical applications

- Ni80Cr20: Extensively used in industrial applications
- 0Cr23Al5: Extensively used in consumer applications
